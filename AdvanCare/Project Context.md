# advanCARE: AI-enabled Healthcare Management Platform for Grassroots Communities

> AI-enabled healthcare management platform for grassroots communities — intergenerational care ecosystem bridging youth and elderly care services in Cagayan Province — "No Farmer, Fisher, and Barangay Left Behind."

## 1. Vision / Context

advanCARE is pitching an innovative **AI-enabled healthcare management platform for grassroots communities** — an intergenerational care ecosystem that bridges the gap between youth and elderly care services. Utilizing a comprehensive [[Fishbone Diagram.jpeg]] to map out and dissect market vulnerabilities, we have engineered our solution by fusing the operational breakthroughs of industry leaders like [Honor](https://www.honorcare.com/) (tech-enabled senior care platforms), [Homage](https://www.homage.com.my/) (on-demand holistic healthcare matching), and [Auxilio Care](https://www.auxiliocare.com/) (highly localized, complex bespoke support).

Crucially, our business model is built for absolute **financial self-sustainability**, operating through private-market revenue loops rather than dependence on government subsidies.

Agriculture and fisheries play a vital role in the economic and social development of Cagayan Province, Philippines. Many families depend on farming and fishing as their primary source of income and livelihood. Inclusive human development aims to ensure all members of society, regardless of age, physical condition, or socioeconomic status, have equal opportunities to participate in community activities and access essential services.

For aging farmers and fishers, this means appropriate healthcare, social protection, livelihood support, and opportunities to share knowledge with younger generations. For persons with special needs, it involves creating an accessible, supportive, and inclusive environment where they can develop abilities, participate in community life, and enjoy equal opportunities.

Fishbone head: **Farmers, Fishers, People with Special Needs** — addressing the full spectrum of needs, from access and affordability to specialized care and insurance gaps, for a healthier, more inclusive community.

## 2. Problem Owner

Aging farmers, fishers, and low-income families of people with special needs in Cagayan who struggle to access affordable, reliable care and wellness support.

These owners both experience the issue daily and manage its consequences within the household — arranging care, absorbing costs, and adjusting livelihood work around caregiving gaps. Barangay health workers, families, communities, and local government units share secondary management responsibility for coordination.

## 3. User / Beneficiary

Target Market: Barangay Residents.

Who directly benefits if the problem is solved:

- Aging farmers and fishers
- People with special needs and their families
- Family caregivers and youth community caregivers gaining livelihood
- Underserved barangay communities, including long-distance family members needing updates and peace of mind

## 4. What Is Happening Now

Families often find caregivers and healthcare providers independently, while community resources and available services may be difficult to access or coordinate.

Current state, mapped to [[Fishbone Diagram.jpeg]]:

- **People:** word-of-mouth search for trusted, screened caregivers; no reliable skill-to-need matching
- **Technology / Coordination:** no simple booking/scheduling; no shared family updates and care records; visits and payments not coordinated in one place
- **Health:** personal care, nursing, therapy sourced separately; weak support for dementia/chronic conditions; loss of continuity after hospital discharge
- **Access:** services concentrated in towns/cities; transport and distance block rural use; hard to find nearby qualified providers
- **Cost:** opaque rates; families balance care needs against tight farm/fisher budgets alone
- **Family:** work vs. caregiving balanced informally; long-distance members lack visibility; no structured respite/guidance
- **Insurance / Coverage:** non-emergency HCare transportation not covered; pharmacy needs outside DoH Yakap (e.g., vitamins, supplements) paid out-of-pocket; out-of-hospital triage limited or unavailable

## 5. Pain Point

Limited access to affordable, reliable, and specialized care, especially in remote communities where transportation and financial constraints create barriers.

Where the process fails / slows / duplicates / loses data:

- **Fails:** remote barangays lack nearby qualified providers; post-discharge continuity breaks; triage outside hospital unavailable
- **Slows down:** manual independent search + no central booking delays matching; transport/distance adds hours
- **Duplicates work:** families repeat intake, search, and payment for each episode; no reusable care record
- **Loses data:** no shared updates between family, caregiver, clinic, and barangay; long-distance family left out
- **Cost barrier:** professional care unaffordable without transparent hourly rates/packages; insurance/Yakap gaps shift cost to families (transport, vitamins/supplements)

## 6. Root Cause

Aligned 1:1 to the 7 fishbone bones — what causes and reinforces the problem:

1. **People / Caregivers:** shortage of trusted, screened caregivers; poor matching of skills to each senior's needs; lack of availability and continuity
2. **Cost / Affordability:** professional care priced beyond family budgets; rates/packages not transparent; no mechanism to balance care needs with income
3. **Access / Geography:** services not expanded across communities; transport and distance barriers; scarcity of nearby qualified providers
4. **Technology / Coordination:** no simple booking and scheduling; no system for family updates and care records; visits and payments fragmented across places
5. **Health / Specialized Care:** gaps in personal care, nursing, therapy; insufficient dementia and chronic-condition support; no continuity after hospital discharge
6. **Family / Caregiving Burden:** difficulty balancing work and caregiving; no support for long-distance family members; lack of respite, guidance, and peace of mind
7. **Insurance / Service Coverage:** non-emergency HCare transportation not covered; pharmacy outside DoH Yakap not covered; out-of-hospital triage limited or unavailable

Reinforcing loop: distance raises cost, cost suppresses demand, low demand suppresses caregiver supply, fragmentation + coverage gaps prevent pooling of supply and continuity.

## 7. Evidence

- Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA), Cagayan Province recorded 5,040 deaths in 2020. Of these, 2,836 deaths (56.3%) had no recorded medical attendant. This highlights a potential concern regarding access to medical attention in the province and supports the need to investigate healthcare accessibility for aging farmers/fishers and people with special needs.
- 31.6% of farmers experienced poverty in 2018 according to PSA. This highlights the financial challenges faced by farmers and the potential difficulty of affording healthcare and wellness services.
- Fishbone qualitative evidence: the 7 categories above were observed as repeated failure points — trust/screening, rate transparency, transport, booking/records, specialized/continuity care, family burden, and Yakap/transport/triage coverage gaps.
- Baseline: aging farmers/fishers and persons with special needs face limited access to healthcare, social services, financial assistance, livelihood opportunities, and community support.

This connects to AdvanCare by emphasizing the need for affordable, accessible care and wellness support for aging farmers, fishers, and low-income families of people with special needs in Cagayan.

## 8. Impact

If unsolved:

- Reduced access to appropriate care, including personal, nursing, therapy, dementia/chronic, and post-discharge care
- Increased family caregiving burdens, pulling time from farming/fishing livelihood and work
- Potential health risks, including unattended medical need (cf. 56.3% without attendant)
- Greater difficulty participating in community life, weakening family relationships and social inclusion
- Financial strain from out-of-pocket transport, non-Yakap pharmacy, and opaque rates
- Missed opportunity: youth livelihood in care work + intergenerational knowledge transfer from elders

## 9. Success Measures

How improvement will be measured:

A. More aging farmers, fishers, and people with special needs connected to appropriate care — including specialized and post-discharge continuity.
B. Increased access to wellness services in underserved barangays — nearby provider coverage, reduced transport barrier.
C. Affordable services supported by business revenue rather than government subsidies — transparent rates, take-up of packages.
D. Improved family satisfaction and coordination of care — single booking/record/payment, family updates, respite.
E. More communities reached, supporting the goal of "No Farmer, Fisher, and Barangay Left Behind."

Operational proxies from service tiers: SLA adherence (Platinum 1hr city / 3hr rural, Gold 3hr city / 6hr rural, Silver scheduled 24–48hr), repeat use, coverage by barangay, caregiver retention and continuity, post-discharge follow-through rate.
